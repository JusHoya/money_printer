**Astra review — September 6, 2026**

**Verdict: REDIRECT — retain the settlement and evaluation infrastructure, pause broad evolutionary search, and require evidence of executable portfolio profit and capacity before treating this as a path to loan-payment income.**

Reviewed `revival/pleiades-2026-09` at `230ffe4`; deliverables are on `review/astra-2026-09-06`. The bot is technically feasible. A dependable progression from $300 to $2,000/month is **not demonstrated by this project**, and I would not fund the current strategy. This is an absence of sufficient evidence, not a proof that every Kalshi strategy must lose.

I understand the goal is to **grow** the initial $300, not immediately withdraw $2,000 from it. The review therefore evaluates both finding an edge and eventually scaling it.

**The most consequential mistake is treating a positive return per contract as evidence of a scalable income-producing portfolio.** Search fitness omits position quantities, idle capital, competing signals, settlement lockup, and portfolio constraints. Those omissions matter more than making the search faster. The revival document's proposed $100–300/month on $350 also remains an unsupported income target; it implies roughly 29–86% monthly profit relative to starting capital.

**Priority findings and improvements**

1. **P1 — Add a chronological portfolio evaluator before any further search.** In `src/factory/fitness.py:259`, the kernel averages trade returns within each active date, then averages those date means. This is a legitimate statistic, but not account PnL. A synthetic example: one contract earns $0.10 on one day; 100 contracts each lose $0.02 on another. Mean daily per-contract return is +$0.04, while total profit is −$1.90. This is an illustrative counterexample, not an allegation that the recorded OOS sample loses: its reconstructed one-contract-per-opportunity total is +$3.0765.

   Preserve date clustering for uncertainty, but compute daily **sums of actual sized cashflows**, including zero-trade days. Replay fee rounding at actual quantities, signal ordering, open positions, blocked collateral, settlement releases, drawdown stops, and competing city slots. Report dollars/month, drawdown, capital utilization, fill quantity and turnover alongside per-contract edge. Keep existing fitness for historical comparability; introduce any new selection objective under a new family registration.

2. **P1 — Fix the mismatch between research admissibility and the execution policy.** `src/factory/features.py:226` checks a model-edge/fee predicate; it does not implement all runtime controls. `src/core/risk_manager.py:458` blends a 50% historical prior with model confidence, giving `p = 0.30 + 0.40*confidence` before sufficient history. Consequently, even perfect confidence cannot size a purchase above 70¢ on the zero-maker-fee weather path.

   A 50% win probability is not a neutral *edge* prior for an 84¢ contract. The rule embeds a strong price preference and later blends a strategy-wide win rate across potentially different price bands. That is a design issue independent of whether this particular genome is good. However, removing it would not establish an edge, and the owner's decision rejecting protected risk changes still governs. Do not seed live win records with selected backtest outcomes. If reconsidered later, compare a separately specified price-conditioned uncertainty model and a tightly capped fixed-size paper policy across price bands and null controls, before changing protected code.

   Additional execution mismatch: `src/strategies/genome_strategy.py:887` marks a market traded before downstream acceptance; the city-slot check in `src/bots/mixins.py:325` permits one concurrent weather position per city/type, and the runtime has a 10-second trade interval. A rejected emission can therefore consume its opportunity. Define emitted, pending, accepted and filled states separately; replay the same policy offline. Changing retry behavior changes the strategy and requires new parity evidence and registration. Adding only the cold-start Boolean to v2 is incomplete.

3. **P1 — The fill study does not validate its claimed bound.** `scripts/measure_fill_realism.py:393` calls next-poll drift an upper bound on 20-second drift. It is not: an ask can move 80¢ → 90¢ at 10 seconds → 80¢ at 40 seconds. Next-poll drift is zero while the 20-second excursion is 10¢. The report has no primary-window observations and substitutes 156 later quote comparisons from one day. It also selects market/hour observations rather than conditioning on the genome's actual executable signals and quantities.

   Treat 1¢ as an unvalidated assumption. Record exchange-timestamped depth around actual decisions, quote age, latency, rejected opportunities and quantity available at the limit. Missing depth or observation windows should produce unknown/unfillable cases, not evidence of cheap fills. Repeated unchanged dashboard quotes do not establish order-book liquidity. This remains necessary even for takers; a maker study additionally needs a defensible queue/fill model.

4. **P2 — More search does not solve the sample-size problem.** The committed pooled active-date statistics have standard deviation about $0.3255. Under an illustrative IID normal approximation, detecting a 4¢ mean with 80% power and a two-sided 5% test requires about **520 active dates**: `ceil(((1.96+0.841621)*sd/0.04)^2)`. At an assumed SD of $0.19 it requires 178. These are planning sensitivities, not calibrated power guarantees for a bootstrap-selected, dependent strategy. Serial weather regimes and selection can make effective information lower.

   Preserve date clustering; relabeling four correlated cities as four independent observations does not manufacture evidence. Add blocked-date sensitivity analysis. Preserve ratified thresholds and existing failure decisions. A failed underpowered test closes that deployment case; it does not prove all weather markets permanently untradeable. Likewise, the ratified absence-of-GEFS route is a governance assumption, not new empirical evidence that forecast-source sensitivity disappeared.

5. **P2 — Align fees and risk semantics across layers.** The fitness uses entry fees with a default 20-contract fee calculation (`src/factory/fees.py`); admissibility uses two taker fees at one contract; Kelly sizing uses two maker fees (`src/core/risk_manager.py:440`). Some differences intentionally reflect conservative hypothetical exits, but the full policy needs one documented execution/holding convention and comparisons at actual order sizes.

   The official July 7 schedule specifies the general taker coefficient 0.07, maker coefficient 0.0175 with a default maker multiplier of zero, and zero multipliers for KXBTCY/KXETHY. This updates the expansion memo's description of the annual fee holiday as supported only by an API field. Fees still do not establish alpha or turnover. [Kalshi fee schedule](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf).

   Current API documentation also distinguishes account balance precision and per-order rounding accumulators. The repo's cent-ceiling model is not a complete implementation of those mechanics. Whole-cent examples below use the repo convention; reconcile future actual fills before claiming sub-cent accuracy. This can affect small orders, but is not evidence that updating fees rescues the strategy. [Kalshi fee rounding](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/fee_rounding).

**The income and scaling arithmetic**

At a hypothetical **4¢ net edge**, $2,000 requires **50,000 contracts/month**, or about 1,667/day. At 3¢ it requires roughly 66,667/month. Neither edge nor that executable volume has been demonstrated.

For four cities, one filled entry per city per day, 50 contracts per entry and 4¢ net, the arithmetic is **$240/month**. At the deployed fixture's 130 opportunities/69 dates, the same generous 50-contract assumption gives **$113/month**. These are conditional illustrations, not hard exchange-wide ceilings or profit forecasts. They assume an edge, complete fills and daily capital release, and ignore additional rejects. The current code's 50-contract cap and one concurrent city/type slot limit this lane even after adding capital.

At $300, the seed-stage 10% allocation ceiling buys at most 35 contracts at 84¢; at $350 it buys 41, before fee/cash checks. The corresponding four-city illustrations become $168 and $196.80/month. **Cold-start Kelly actually rejects these 84¢ purchases**, so these numbers must not be used as current achievable targets.

Compounding alone also takes time:

| Assumed constant net monthly return | Capital producing $2,000 average monthly profit | Months from $300, no additions or withdrawals |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1% | $200,000 | 654 |
| 2% | $100,000 | 294 |
| 5% | $40,000 | 101 |
| 10% | $20,000 | 45 |

These are mathematical scenarios, **not expected Kalshi returns**. Even 5% every month is a demanding assumption. Variable returns, capacity saturation, costs and withdrawals can materially worsen the path. For comparison, an assumed 5% return plus $300 contributed at each month-end reaches $40,000 in approximately 41 months. Funding can change the timeline, but cannot create an edge.

Average trading profit also differs from reliable spendable income. A loan-payment plan needs allowance for losing months, operating costs, taxes and a separate cash reserve. Three positive live months alone would not establish withdrawal reliability. If covering loans soon is the priority, earned income or external capital accumulation is a more controllable funding mechanism; the bot should remain a bounded research project until its account-level economics are measured.

**Recommended alternative and first falsifiable test**

Redirect from unconstrained rule evolution to **one fixed weather rule evaluated as an executable portfolio**. This reuses the strongest existing assets and adds no paid data feed. It is a cheaper way to decide whether to continue, not a claim that a replacement strategy is already profitable. No alternative in the reviewed evidence meets that stronger standard.

For this alternative, capacity is bounded by the same $300/$350 arithmetic above. A purchase at 84¢ for 20 contracts costs $16.80 plus $0.19 under the repo fee convention, and needs a true win probability above 84.95% to break even when held to settlement. Settlement does not require a second market trade. A 50¢, 20-contract entry costs $10 plus $0.35. Spreads, delays and capital lockup remain material even when fees are small.

If fixed-rule weather fails, do not automatically build another engine. The only inexpensive execution variant worth keeping on a shortlist is the already documented one-sided maker residue, **if suitable development depth/tape exists**. Register it as a separate family and reject touch-only fills, include maker fees where applicable, and demand positive realized date-clustered results after conservative fills. Maker status can reduce fees, but adverse selection can consume the saving. Neither its profitability nor capacity is established. Annual crypto's long holding period and scarce independent settlements make it a poor fit for validating monthly income; mention markets would require new corpus and settlement work before making an edge claim.
